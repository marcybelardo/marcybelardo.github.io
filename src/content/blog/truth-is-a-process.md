In a past life, I mainly worked in SEO and advertising. These are typical career paths for someone trying to make a living as a writer. There's plenty of space to be creative, or to at least flex your ability to take on new points of view without absorbing them. If you want to get good at writing fiction, it pays to know how to write things outside of your interest, towards audiences that you are not a part of. I found myself moving away from these fields in time and towards technology, mostly because I couldn't shake the feeling that I was lying constantly. To write a fictional character is one thing, but to actively try and sell a product I don't use or even think is bad is another. Marketing and deception are not necessarily synonymous, but they are cousins, at the very least.

I was still working in SEO when ChatGPT arrived on the field. Slowly, it became more of a household name and the job-destruction marketing started to filter out through the media and into our lives. Outside of coding, it seemed like my career in SEO copywriting was the most LLM-shaped career out there. It's a kind of writing that isn't strongly concerned with quality or factual correctness. As the name suggest, you optimize you text for search. Because you do that, you let other quality markers off on the wayside. I was worried when AI got better because it stuck at a fundamental understanding of mine that in industries where quality is a secondary concern, then AI is a very justifiable cost over employees. Time has passed and my view has normalized around AI. I still believe that businesses that don't value quality would prefer AI agents over human workers, but I also believe that those businesses will fail. If anything, AI only reaches its potential when you put people around it. And where AI finds success is less in the output it generates than in the workflows surrounding it where we must document and control for quality.
